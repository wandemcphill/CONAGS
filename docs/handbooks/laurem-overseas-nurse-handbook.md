# Conags Care
## Overseas Registered Nurse Handbook

**Audience:** Internationally recruited Registered Nurses joining Conags Care in Scotland.

**Purpose:** Provide a practical, nurse-specific guide from offer and sponsorship through arrival, NMC registration, clinical induction and settlement in the UK.

**Status:** Employer handbook template. It must be reviewed against the nurse's actual offer, immigration permission, NMC position and current law before issue.

---

## 1. Welcome to Conags and Scotland

Welcome to Conags Care. We recognise that international recruitment is more than filling a vacancy. Moving country, entering a new health and care system and building a new professional life requires structured support.

Conags will provide an organised pre-arrival, arrival, professional and pastoral support pathway. International recruits should have a named contact and should be able to raise concerns about employment, immigration, accommodation, finances, professional registration, safety or wellbeing.

## 2. Your international nurse pathway

Your CONAGS candidate record should show the stage you are at:

1. Recruitment and eligibility
2. Conditional offer
3. Sponsorship assessment
4. NMC application and registration pathway
5. Visa and travel preparation
6. Pre-arrival orientation
7. Arrival and settlement
8. Corporate and clinical induction
9. NMC registration completion where outstanding
10. Full deployment into the registered nurse workforce
11. Ongoing professional development and review

## 3. Ethical international recruitment

Conags will conduct international recruitment in accordance with the applicable UK and Scottish Code of Practice principles. Recruitment must be transparent, fair and free from exploitation.

Workers must receive clear information about the employer, role, location, pay, working hours, accommodation, deductions, immigration arrangements and any costs they are expected to bear.

A UK-based recruitment organisation must not charge a job-finding fee for finding employment. Sponsorship-related charges that the sponsor is prohibited from passing to the worker must not be recovered from the worker through disguised deductions or repayment arrangements.

Conags should maintain a record of recruitment suppliers and assess them against the relevant ethical recruitment requirements.

## 4. Your employment contract

Your employment contract is the binding source for your job title, pay, hours, holidays, notice and other contractual terms. This handbook provides operational guidance and does not replace the contract.

You should never be asked to sign a document you do not understand. Ask Conags for clarification before accepting a material employment term.

## 5. Sponsorship and Health and Care Worker visa

Where Conags is the sponsor, the worker must meet the current Skilled Worker/Health and Care Worker requirements for the sponsored role.

The Certificate of Sponsorship is an electronic record with a reference number. The sponsored worker must make the relevant visa application within the applicable timeframe.

Visa policy changes frequently. Your recruiter must confirm the current rules for your nationality, occupation code, salary, dependants and application route before giving immigration instructions.

From 8 January 2026, first-time Skilled Worker applicants under the updated rules may be required to demonstrate English at B2 level unless an applicable exception applies. Do not rely on an old English-language checklist.

## 6. NMC registration

Internationally trained nurses must complete the NMC's applicable overseas registration process and satisfy its requirements for identity, qualification, English language evidence, health and character, professional experience and the Test of Competence where required.

Where the Test of Competence route applies, the process includes the CBT and OSCE stages. Nurses must successfully complete the required stages before joining the NMC register.

The NMC process and fees may change. The current NMC portal and published guidance are the source of truth.

### Conags NMC tracking

The CONAGS workforce record should track:

- NMC application status
- qualification assessment status
- English evidence status
- CBT status
- OSCE booking/status
- NMC registration number once granted
- registration expiry/renewal information where applicable
- conditions or restrictions, if any

## 7. Before you travel

Conags should provide a structured pre-arrival pack containing:

- formal offer and employment contract
- sponsorship information and Certificate of Sponsorship reference where appropriate
- visa checklist and official links
- travel and airport-arrival instructions
- contact details for the Conags arrival coordinator
- temporary accommodation details where supplied
- local area guide
- expected weather and clothing information
- UK electrical plug/adaptor information
- expected costs during the first month
- payroll and bank-account information
- information about NHS and healthcare access
- NMC pathway information
- mandatory training and induction expectations
- emergency contacts
- safeguarding and professional expectations
- information on unions, professional organisations, embassy/high commission services and community networks

## 8. Travel and airport arrival

Before departure, confirm the agreed arrival date, airport, transport arrangement, accommodation address and emergency contact route.

If Conags is providing collection, follow the supplied instructions. Do not travel to an unknown address or transfer money to an unknown person claiming to be a Conags representative.

Keep your passport and immigration evidence secure throughout travel.

## 9. Your first 72 hours in Scotland

Your arrival process should prioritise safety, accommodation and communication.

### Day 1

Confirm safe arrival, accommodation, emergency contact information, access to food and essential supplies, and the next scheduled contact with Conags.

### Day 2

Complete local orientation, confirm work start arrangements, obtain information on transport, local shops, healthcare and other immediate needs.

### Day 3

Confirm payroll information, bank-account arrangements, immigration/right-to-work evidence, induction schedule and any outstanding professional registration tasks.

## 10. Accommodation

Where Conags provides or arranges accommodation, the worker should receive clear written information about the address, duration, rent, deposits, utilities, deductions and tenancy conditions.

A tied accommodation arrangement must not be treated as an informal obligation. Where accommodation is provided under a tenancy arrangement, the terms should be documented.

Employees remain free to obtain independent accommodation where they choose and lawful immigration/work arrangements allow.

## 11. Money, payroll and first salary

Your first salary may not arrive immediately after arrival depending on payroll cut-off dates. Conags should tell you the expected pay date before you travel.

Budget for:

- food
- local transport
- mobile phone
- clothing and work items
- accommodation and utilities where applicable
- household essentials
- professional registration or examination costs where you are responsible for them

Any salary advance or employee loan must have written terms showing the amount and lawful repayment method.

## 12. Bank account and National Insurance

Conags may help you understand how to open a UK bank account and how payroll operates.

Where a National Insurance number is needed, Conags should signpost you to the official government process. Do not pay an unverified person to “activate” or “release” a National Insurance number.

## 13. Right to work and eVisa

Most non-British and non-Irish workers will use digital immigration status and may prove their right to work through a Home Office share code where eligible.

Conags will perform the required right-to-work check and retain the evidence required by law.

You should keep your UKVI account details secure and tell Conags promptly about any change that could affect your work permission.

## 14. GP, dentist and healthcare

Conags should signpost new recruits to local GP registration, dental services and urgent/emergency healthcare arrangements.

Learn the distinction between emergency services, urgent care, NHS 24/111 where applicable in Scotland, GP services and routine appointments.

Do not delay urgent medical care because you are new to the UK.

## 15. Scottish healthcare and care-system orientation

Your previous practice experience is valuable, but the Scottish system may use different terminology, policies, referral pathways, documentation practices and professional expectations.

Your induction should cover the local service model, person-centred practice, safeguarding, documentation, medicines management, infection prevention, escalation, incident reporting and professional boundaries.

## 16. Clinical governance and patient safety

Patient/service-user safety is the priority. Escalate deterioration, safeguarding concerns, medication incidents, errors, complaints and other risks promptly.

Practise within your competence and authorised scope. Ask for support where a procedure, medication, device or clinical situation is unfamiliar.

## 17. NMC Code and professional conduct

Once registered, you must comply with the NMC Code and ongoing professional requirements.

Even before full registration, where you are employed in a supervised or pre-registration capacity, you must follow Conags' role restrictions and supervision arrangements.

Never represent yourself as an NMC-registered nurse before registration is confirmed.

## 18. Cultural transition

Workplace communication can differ from the system you trained in. Conags should provide support with:

- Scottish and UK workplace language
- common abbreviations and terminology
- documentation conventions
- escalation culture
- feedback and supervision
- speaking up about unsafe care
- equality and inclusion
- religious and dietary needs
- local customs and social expectations

The aim is adaptation without requiring workers to give up their culture, identity or beliefs.

## 19. Buddy and mentor support

Conags should assign a named buddy or mentor during the settling-in period where operationally possible.

The buddy is not a substitute for formal clinical supervision. Their role is to help the new recruit understand the workplace, local routines, transport, terminology and available support.

## 20. Family and dependants

Immigration rules for dependants vary by route, occupation, application date and other factors. Conags must not make a blanket promise that dependants can accompany every worker.

Candidates should be directed to current GOV.UK rules before making travel or school arrangements.

Where family members accompany the employee, the welcome programme should cover schooling, healthcare, accommodation, transport and community support.

## 21. Schools and childcare

Where applicable, Conags should provide signposting for local schools, childcare and local-authority services. Families should confirm admission dates and eligibility directly with the relevant council or school.

## 22. Community, faith and social networks

Settling into a new country can be easier with a support network. Conags should provide information on relevant local community groups, faith communities, professional organisations, trade unions and diaspora networks without requiring workers to join any particular organisation.

## 23. Fraud, scams and exploitation

Never pay a third party claiming they can fix a visa, release a CoS, accelerate NMC registration, guarantee a job or recover money from immigration authorities.

Be especially cautious with requests for urgent payment, personal banking details, passport copies sent to unknown addresses or recruitment fees disguised as “administration”.

Use official GOV.UK, NMC and Conags channels for verification.

## 24. Equality, dignity and freedom from harassment

International nurses have the same workplace legal protections as other employees. Conags expects an inclusive working environment and has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment, discrimination, exploitation or retaliation for raising legitimate concerns.

## 25. Financial and employment rights

Before accepting deductions, accommodation charges, repayment clauses or changes to duties, ask for a written explanation.

Your wages must comply with applicable employment and immigration requirements. Conags should be transparent about deductions and ensure they are lawful.

## 26. What to do if something goes wrong

### Immigration problem
Contact Conags' designated sponsor/HR contact promptly and use official UKVI information.

### NMC problem
Contact the NMC through your secure NMC account or published official channels.

### Accommodation problem
Notify the accommodation contact/Conags and, where appropriate, the landlord or local authority.

### Pay problem
Raise the issue with Conags payroll/HR and retain your payslips and timesheet records.

### Safeguarding concern
Use Conags' safeguarding process and emergency/statutory channels where required.

### Recruitment exploitation
Tell Conags and use the appropriate official reporting/support route. You should not be penalised for raising a genuine concern.

## 27. First six weeks at Conags

The first six weeks should include:

**Week 1:** arrival, safety, accommodation, identity/right-to-work, payroll, local orientation and corporate induction.

**Week 2:** supervised workplace orientation, care-system differences, documentation, safeguarding, infection prevention and escalation.

**Week 3:** clinical competency review, role-specific training and buddy/mentor check-in.

**Week 4:** NMC progress review, professional development needs and welfare review.

**Week 5:** independent-practice readiness assessment where applicable.

**Week 6:** formal settling-in review covering clinical practice, workload, accommodation, finances, wellbeing and career goals.

## 28. Ongoing career development

International recruitment must not become a career dead-end. Conags should provide access to training, supervision, professional development and progression opportunities equivalent to those available to comparable domestic staff.

Career conversations should cover clinical interests, specialist training, leadership, education, further qualifications and longer-term development.

## 29. Important official resources

Use current official sources for immigration, professional regulation and public services. Key resources include GOV.UK immigration guidance, the NMC, Scottish Government international recruitment guidance, Disclosure Scotland, SSSC where relevant, and local NHS/Scottish public-service information.
