## 1. Welcome to Conags Care

Welcome to Conags Care Group Limited.

This Staff Handbook provides a clear framework for the standards of conduct, care, safety, confidentiality and professionalism expected during your employment or engagement with Conags.

It is designed to help you understand:

- what Conags expects from you;
- what you can expect from Conags;
- how to work safely and professionally;
- how to raise concerns and obtain support; and
- where to find the procedures that apply to your particular role.

The handbook should be read alongside your employment contract and job description. It does not replace your contract or any applicable law, professional requirement, care plan or company policy.

Where a role is subject to professional registration, immigration conditions, statutory checks or role-specific requirements, those requirements also apply.

---

## 2. Our standards of care and conduct

Every member of the Conags team is expected to act with professionalism, integrity, reliability and respect.

Our work is centred on the safety, dignity, rights and wellbeing of the people who use our services.

You are expected to:

- treat service users, colleagues, families, visitors and partner organisations with courtesy and respect;
- communicate honestly and professionally;
- work within your training, competence and authorised responsibilities;
- follow lawful instructions and applicable company procedures;
- protect confidential information;
- raise concerns promptly when something is unsafe, inappropriate or unclear; and
- maintain professional boundaries at all times.

---

## 3. Person-centred care

People receiving care or support must be treated as individuals.

You should respect each service user's dignity, privacy, preferences, communication needs, independence, culture, beliefs and lawful choices.

Care should be delivered in accordance with the applicable care plan and agreed support arrangements.

You must not:

- humiliate, threaten, intimidate or unnecessarily restrict a service user;
- make decisions on a person's behalf without proper authority;
- ignore a person's communication needs or reasonable preferences; or
- provide care in a way that is inconsistent with their agreed plan or your level of competence.

When you are uncertain, seek guidance from the appropriate manager or senior colleague before proceeding.

---

## 4. Safeguarding

Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility.

You must remain alert to possible abuse, neglect, exploitation, coercion or other risks to children and adults who may be at risk.

Concerns may include, for example:

- physical, emotional or sexual abuse;
- neglect or poor treatment;
- financial abuse or exploitation;
- discriminatory or degrading treatment;
- coercion, intimidation or controlling behaviour;
- unexplained injuries or significant changes in behaviour;
- unsafe care practices; or
- concerns about the conduct of another worker.

Report safeguarding concerns immediately through the applicable Conags procedure.

Do not conduct your own investigation, confront an alleged perpetrator or promise a person that information will remain secret. Record and report the facts through the correct route.

Where there is an immediate risk of serious harm, use the appropriate emergency or statutory service without delay.

---

## 5. Working within your competence

You must only carry out duties that you are trained, competent, authorised and permitted to perform.

This includes respecting any restrictions arising from:

- your job description;
- supervision requirements;
- training or competency assessments;
- professional registration;
- immigration or right-to-work conditions; and
- instructions contained in care or assignment documentation.

A busy shift is never a reason to perform a task that you are not competent to undertake.

Ask for assistance whenever you are unsure.

---

## 6. Professional boundaries

Professional relationships must remain appropriate and focused on the person's care, support or service.

You must not use your position to obtain personal, financial or other advantages from service users or their families.

Do not:

- borrow or lend money with service users;
- enter inappropriate financial arrangements;
- accept inappropriate gifts or benefits;
- use service-user relationships for private business or personal gain;
- share unnecessary personal information; or
- use personal social-media relationships to bypass professional boundaries.

Any gifts, conflicts of interest or unusual requests should be handled in accordance with the relevant Conags procedure.

---

## 7. Attendance, punctuality and rota responsibilities

Reliable attendance is essential to safe service delivery.

You must:

- attend scheduled shifts and assignments on time;
- arrive prepared to begin work at the required start time;
- follow the approved rota and assignment arrangements;
- record attendance and working time accurately where required; and
- tell the designated Conags contact as soon as reasonably possible if you cannot attend or are delayed.

You must never ask another person to record attendance or working time on your behalf.

Absence, sickness and leave must be managed through the applicable Conags procedure.

---

## 8. Rota, workforce platform and staff portal

Where instructed, the Conags workforce platform and Staff Portal are the official channels for operational information such as:

- rota and assignment information;
- attendance and timesheets;
- approved leave;
- onboarding tasks;
- staff communications;
- required documents;
- training and compliance actions; and
- other workforce information made available to you.

Keep your login credentials secure. Do not share passwords, authentication codes or portal access with another person.

Use company systems responsibly and only for authorised purposes.

---

## 9. Recording information and care documentation

Accurate records are an essential part of safe care.

Where you are responsible for records, entries must be:

- accurate;
- timely;
- factual;
- clear and professional; and
- completed using the approved system or documentation.

Do not falsify, backdate or deliberately omit information.

If you make an error, follow the applicable correction procedure. Never alter a record to conceal an incident or mistake.

---

## 10. Confidentiality and information governance

You may have access to confidential information about service users, colleagues and Conags.

Confidential information must only be accessed, used or shared for legitimate work purposes and in accordance with applicable law and Conags procedures.

You must not:

- photograph confidential records or screens without authorisation;
- copy confidential information to personal accounts;
- send work information through unauthorised channels;
- discuss service-user information in public places; or
- post confidential information, photographs or workplace details on social media.

These responsibilities continue after your employment or engagement ends.

---

## 11. Digital conduct and social media

Use digital services professionally.

Your personal social-media activity must not compromise service-user confidentiality, professional boundaries, workplace safety or Conags' legitimate business interests.

Do not represent personal opinions as official Conags statements.

Any online communication connected with a service user, colleague, care incident or confidential company matter must follow the applicable Conags procedure.

---

## 12. Health, safety and infection prevention

You are expected to contribute actively to a safe working environment.

Follow all applicable requirements relating to:

- infection prevention and control;
- personal protective equipment;
- moving and assisting;
- fire safety;
- workplace hazards;
- safe use of equipment;
- lone working where applicable;
- manual handling;
- emergency procedures; and
- occupational health requirements relevant to your role.

Report unsafe conditions promptly.

Do not knowingly ignore a hazard or continue an activity that presents an unreasonable risk of harm.

---

## 13. Medicines and delegated tasks

Where your role involves supporting medicines or other delegated care tasks, you must follow the training, competency, care-plan and organisational requirements that apply to you.

Never undertake a medicines-related or delegated task outside your authorised scope.

Report medicines errors, omissions, refusals, suspected adverse reactions and other relevant incidents promptly through the approved process.

---

## 14. Incidents, accidents and near misses

Report accidents, injuries, near misses, safeguarding concerns, medication incidents, complaints and other serious risks as soon as reasonably possible.

Reports should describe what happened accurately and factually.

You must not conceal an incident, alter records to minimise its significance or discourage another person from reporting a concern.

Prompt reporting allows Conags to respond, protect people and take appropriate follow-up action.

---

## 15. Equality, dignity and inclusion

Conags is committed to respectful and inclusive working relationships.

Discrimination, harassment, bullying, victimisation, intimidation and exploitation are not acceptable.

You should treat people fairly and respectfully regardless of their personal characteristics, background, nationality, disability, beliefs or other protected or personal circumstances.

Concerns should be raised through the appropriate management, grievance or safeguarding route.

---

## 16. Professional appearance and workplace behaviour

Your appearance and behaviour should be appropriate to your role, service environment and any specific uniform or dress requirements.

Maintain good personal hygiene and present yourself in a manner consistent with safe and professional care.

At work you must not behave in a way that threatens safety, disrupts care, intimidates others or damages professional trust.

Alcohol or illegal drug use must never place service users, colleagues or workplace safety at risk. Follow applicable company requirements concerning alcohol, drugs and fitness for work.

---

## 17. Training, supervision and competence

Mandatory training and role-specific learning are part of safe practice.

You must complete required training within the specified timescales and participate in competency assessments, supervision, induction and review processes relevant to your role.

Where a competency has not yet been confirmed, you must not independently perform the activity unless the applicable supervision arrangements expressly permit it.

Conags may require refresher training where regulations, procedures, service requirements or your responsibilities change.

---

## 18. Raising concerns and speaking up

You should raise concerns early rather than allow an unsafe or inappropriate situation to continue.

Concerns may relate to:

- safeguarding;
- service-user safety;
- staffing or unsafe working conditions;
- discrimination or harassment;
- pay or working hours;
- professional conduct;
- confidentiality or data handling;
- immigration or right-to-work matters;
- accommodation arrangements where applicable;
- recruitment or employment practices; or
- any other serious workplace concern.

Use the appropriate Conags manager, HR, safeguarding, grievance or whistleblowing route.

You should not be discouraged from raising a genuine concern.

---

## 19. Complaints, grievance and disciplinary matters

Conags maintains formal processes for handling complaints, grievances and conduct concerns.

Where a matter requires investigation, staff are expected to cooperate honestly and professionally.

Nothing in this handbook removes any right or procedure provided by your employment contract or applicable law.

Specific procedures will be communicated separately where they apply to your role or circumstances.

---

## 20. Pay, timesheets and deductions

Your pay, working hours, holiday entitlement and contractual benefits are governed primarily by your employment contract and applicable law.

Where you use timesheets or the workforce platform, record working time accurately and submit information within the required timescale.

Raise suspected payroll errors promptly.

Where deductions, accommodation charges, salary advances or other recoverable amounts apply, you should receive the relevant terms or information separately. Do not rely on verbal promises where a written document is required.

---

## 21. Immigration and right-to-work responsibilities

Where your employment is subject to immigration or right-to-work conditions, you must comply with those conditions throughout your employment.

You must promptly inform Conags where a change to your immigration status, permission, passport, work authorisation or other relevant circumstance could affect your ability to work.

You must not work outside the permission or restrictions applicable to you.

Where Conags has sponsorship responsibilities, the company will manage its own statutory and sponsorship duties separately from your employment obligations.

---

## 22. Accommodation and travel

Where Conags provides or arranges accommodation or travel support, the specific terms will be provided separately.

Before accepting an arrangement, review the applicable written information about:

- accommodation;
- rent or other charges;
- deposits;
- utilities;
- deductions;
- travel arrangements;
- temporary accommodation periods; and
- any applicable notice or repayment terms.

Do not assume that accommodation or travel is free unless the written terms state that it is.

---

## 23. Wellbeing and support

Your wellbeing matters.

Speak to your manager or designated Conags contact if you are struggling with workload, settling into a new role, communication, accommodation, finances, professional responsibilities or another issue affecting your ability to work safely.

For urgent medical or emergency concerns, use the appropriate healthcare or emergency service.

Conags may provide or signpost additional support depending on your role and circumstances.

---

## 24. Professional development and progression

Conags expects staff to maintain the skills and knowledge required for their roles and to participate in appropriate development opportunities.

Depending on your position, this may include:

- additional training;
- competency development;
- supervision;
- professional registration requirements;
- career discussions;
- role progression; and
- continuing professional development.

Your manager can explain the opportunities and requirements that apply to your role.

---

## 25. Leaving Conags

Notice, resignation and termination are governed by your employment contract and applicable law.

Before leaving, you may be required to return company property, complete outstanding records, hand over responsibilities and comply with confidentiality obligations.

Where immigration sponsorship or other statutory reporting requirements apply, Conags will manage its responsibilities in accordance with the relevant rules.

Your obligations concerning confidentiality, intellectual property, records and professional conduct may continue after employment ends where applicable.
