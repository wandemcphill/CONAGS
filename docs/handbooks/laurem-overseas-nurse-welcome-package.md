# Conags Care
# Overseas Nurse Welcome Package: Scotland & the UK

**Audience:** Overseas Registered Nurses recruited by Conags Care Group Limited.

This pack is intended to be issued after a nurse accepts the offer and again shortly before travel. It is designed as a practical companion to the employment contract and Overseas Nurse Handbook.

---

## 1. Your Conags arrival contact

**Employer:** Conags Care Group Limited

**Recruitment contact:** recruitment@conagscare.com

**General company contact:** info@conagscare.com

Your candidate portal should display the designated arrival coordinator, out-of-hours emergency contact and accommodation contact for your individual case.

## 2. Before you fly

Confirm the following in your CONAGS portal:

- accepted employment contract
- visa/immigration status
- Certificate of Sponsorship reference where applicable
- travel date and arrival time
- airport and collection instructions where provided
- accommodation address and move-in instructions
- first reporting location
- first day and start time
- emergency contact
- bank/payroll instructions
- NMC status and outstanding actions
- mandatory training schedule
- documents still required
- family/dependant travel arrangements where applicable

Never travel on the assumption that an arrangement is confirmed unless it is recorded in writing by Conags.

## 3. What to pack

### Essential documents

Keep originals or secure official digital copies of:

- passport
- visa/immigration documents or digital status details
- Certificate of Sponsorship information
- employment contract and offer
- NMC correspondence and registration evidence where available
- qualifications and professional certificates
- police/background documentation where requested
- marriage/birth documents where relevant to your own immigration arrangements
- driving licence where applicable
- emergency contacts

### Practical items

- suitable UK clothing for the season
- waterproof outerwear and sensible footwear
- UK Type G plug adaptor
- essential medication in accordance with travel rules
- small amount of accessible funds
- phone and charger
- copies of important documents stored securely

## 4. Money for your first month

Plan for the possibility that your first salary is affected by payroll cut-off dates. Keep enough accessible funds for food, local transport, mobile service and immediate household needs.

Ask Conags in writing about any accommodation, travel or salary-advance arrangements. Where a salary advance is offered, the amount and repayment method should be documented.

## 5. Airport arrival

If Conags is collecting you, follow the instructions in your portal. Confirm the collection person's name or agreed identification method before leaving the airport.

If collection arrangements fail, contact the Conags emergency contact shown in your portal. Do not accept transport from an unknown person claiming to represent Conags.

## 6. Your first day in Scotland

Your first-day checklist should include:

- safe arrival confirmed
- accommodation access confirmed
- emergency contact saved
- local SIM/mobile arrangements
- essential food and household supplies
- transport plan
- first work reporting instructions
- payroll and bank-account next steps
- right-to-work evidence/check
- NMC pathway check
- induction timetable

## 7. Your first week

### Work

Complete corporate induction, safeguarding and safety briefings, mandatory training and local workplace orientation.

### Community

Learn how to reach your workplace, nearest supermarket/pharmacy, GP, dentist, public transport, bank/ATM and emergency services.

### Professional

Confirm your NMC action plan, professional support person and any supervised-practice arrangements.

### Wellbeing

Have a structured check-in with your manager or designated support contact.

## 8. Healthcare in Scotland

For routine healthcare, identify a local GP practice and ask about registration. For urgent health concerns use the appropriate NHS Scotland service; for life-threatening emergencies call 999.

Keep personal health information private and do not rely solely on workplace colleagues for urgent medical care.

## 9. Emergency numbers

**999:** Police, fire, ambulance and other life-threatening emergencies.

**101:** Police non-emergency number.

**111:** NHS urgent health support in Scotland through NHS 24.

For any Conags-specific workplace emergency use the contact displayed in your private portal.

## 10. Transport

Before starting work, plan your journey for normal and early/late shifts. Check public transport times and identify an alternative route.

Do not assume that public transport will run at the same frequency overnight or on public holidays.

If you are expected to drive for work, confirm that your driving licence, insurance and vehicle arrangements are lawful for the required activity.

## 11. Weather and clothing

Scottish weather can change rapidly. Bring layers and waterproof outerwear. Comfortable waterproof footwear is particularly useful during the colder and wetter months.

## 12. Mobile phone and communications

Maintain a reliable UK mobile number once practical. Conags may use approved channels for urgent rota or workforce communication.

Never post confidential patient/service-user information or workplace incidents to public social-media accounts.

## 13. Opening a bank account

Conags can signpost you to mainstream UK bank-account opening routes. You may be asked for identity, address and immigration information.

Do not give banking passwords, one-time passcodes or security answers to Conags staff or recruiters.

## 14. National Insurance

Where a National Insurance number is required, use the official government route. Conags may assist with signposting and payroll onboarding.

Never pay a third party to obtain or “activate” a National Insurance number.

## 15. Understanding your payslip

Your payslip may show gross pay, tax, National Insurance, pension contributions and other lawful deductions.

Ask Conags payroll to explain unfamiliar deductions. Accommodation or other charges should be clearly documented where applicable.

## 16. Accommodation and tenancy

If Conags provides temporary or tied accommodation, obtain the written terms before moving in. Check the rent, deposit, utilities, duration, notice requirements and any salary deductions.

Plan for longer-term accommodation before temporary accommodation ends.

## 17. Family arrival

Where family members are travelling with you, create a separate family checklist covering:

- immigration permissions
- accommodation capacity
- school applications
- childcare
- GP registration
- local transport
- community/faith networks
- emergency contacts
- household budget

Immigration eligibility for dependants must be checked against the current rules for the specific visa route.

## 18. Workplace culture in Scotland

You may notice differences in communication style, hierarchy, escalation, record keeping, professional challenge and feedback. Asking questions is expected and patient/service-user safety comes first.

Conags will support you in learning local terminology, dialect and workplace expectations.

## 19. Raising concerns

You can raise concerns about:

- patient or service-user safety
- safeguarding
- discrimination or harassment
- bullying
- pay
- accommodation arrangements
- recruitment fees or unexpected charges
- immigration compliance
- unsafe staffing
- pressure to work outside your competence

Use the Conags grievance, safeguarding and whistleblowing routes. Genuine concerns should not result in retaliation.

## 20. Protect yourself from recruitment scams

Official UK organisations will not demand an urgent payment from you to “fix” a visa problem. Be cautious of anyone who asks for money to secure a job, release sponsorship paperwork or guarantee NMC registration.

A UK recruitment agency cannot charge you a recruitment/job-finding fee. Sponsorship-related charges that sponsors are prohibited from passing to sponsored workers must not be disguised as recruitment or administration charges.

## 21. Professional organisations and support

You may wish to consider professional or trade-union support relevant to your role. Conags should provide current contact information for relevant organisations and local community networks during induction.

Examples include NMC professional guidance, recognised trade unions and appropriate nursing associations or diaspora networks.
